Perfect is slope 1.000 and intercept 0.000. A slope below 1 means the score is too spread out: the model is over-confident at both ends. A positive intercept means it systematically under-predicts the level of risk. Note that AUC barely moves across the three rows: recalibration is a monotone transform of the score, so it cannot change the ranking and cannot rescue discrimination. Isotonic is not monotone-preserving in the strict sense only because ties are merged, which is why its AUC differs in the fourth decimal.

Brier decomposition

{{ name }} {{ d.brier | fnum(5) }} {{ d.reliability | fnum(5) }} {{ d.resolution | fnum(5) }} {{ d.uncertainty | fnum(5) }} {{ d.binning_residual | fnum(5) }}
Murphy's decomposition: Brier = reliability − resolution + uncertainty. Reliability is the miscalibration penalty, resolution is the credit for separating high-risk from low-risk applicants, and uncertainty is the irreducible variance of the outcome at this base rate. The identity is exact only when forecasts are grouped by identical value; with ten equal-count bins there is a small leftover, reported honestly as the binning residual rather than hidden.
