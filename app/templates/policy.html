Policy simulator

A probability is not a decision. Pick an approval threshold and loss assumptions, and see the approval rate, bad rate, expected loss and profit that follow — on the cohort the model was deployed against and on the cohort six months later.

Synthetic data and illustrative economics. Loss given default, margin and the cost of a false decline are inputs you choose below, not findings. The currency is arbitrary. Nothing here is a claim about any real lender's unit economics.

Expected loss calculator, line by line

principal approved = {{ chosen.principal_approved | fnum(0) }} of which good = {{ chosen.principal_good | fnum(0) }} of which defaulted = {{ chosen.principal_bad | fnum(0) }} revenue = margin {{ econ.margin }} x good principal = {{ chosen.revenue | fnum(0) }} credit loss = lgd {{ econ.lgd }} x defaulted principal = {{ chosen.expected_loss | fnum(0) }} opportunity = {{ econ.fr_cost | fnum(0) }} x {{ chosen.n_rejected_good | fnum(0) }} good applicants declined = {{ chosen.opportunity_cost | fnum(0) }} profit = revenue - credit loss - opportunity = {{ chosen.profit | fnum(0) }}

Deliberately simple arithmetic so it can be checked by hand. It assumes a single-period book, full recovery of principal on good loans, no funding cost, no operational cost and no prepayment. Loss is realised in full at default rather than over a recovery curve.

Read this as an order of magnitude, not a forecast. Both thresholds are chosen by maximising profit on a cohort of a few thousand applications, so both are themselves noisy estimates, and the "fresh" threshold is chosen with hindsight that nobody has in production. The robust claim is the direction and roughly the size: as the population deteriorates the profit-maximising cut-off tightens, and holding the old one costs real money. The fragile claim would be the exact {{ stale.profit_gap | fmoney }}.
